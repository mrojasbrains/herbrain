01so what even is the brain?What is the brain?

the brain is the main character of your whole body, no cap. it takes in everything your senses pick up, figures out what it means, decides what to do and sends out orders, all in like a fraction of a second. your thoughts, memories, feelings and your whole personality? all living rent-free up there.

The brain is the central organ of the nervous system. It receives sensory input, integrates and interprets that information, and generates motor and hormonal responses, typically within milliseconds. It is also the basis of cognition, memory, emotion and personality.

the bossControl center

every move, thought and feeling starts here. your brain slides into every body part's DMs through your nerves.

The brain initiates voluntary movement and regulates the body by sending signals through motor nerves and the endocrine system.

part of the CNSCentral nervous system

brain + spinal cord = the central nervous system (CNS), the main squad. all the nerves going out to the rest of your body are the peripheral nervous system (PNS), aka the side characters.

The brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system (CNS). The cranial and spinal nerves that connect the CNS to the rest of the body form the peripheral nervous system (PNS).

never logs offContinuous activity

even while you sleep it keeps your heart beating, your lungs breathing and your temperature steady. unpaid overtime every single night. a real one.

Even during sleep, the brainstem and hypothalamus maintain heart rate, breathing and body temperature, a process known as homeostasis.

03brain cells 101Cells of the nervous system

your brain runs on two types of cells: neurons, the messengers that send electrical and chemical signals, and glial cells, the support crew that feeds, protects and cleans up after the neurons. the neurons get the clout but the glia are carrying.

Nervous tissue contains two main cell types: neurons, which transmit information using electrical impulses and chemical signals, and glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia), which support, insulate and protect neurons.

  • dendrites are the inbox. they receive messages from other neurons.
  • the cell body has the nucleus and keeps the whole neuron alive.
  • the axon is the send button, a long cable that passes the message on.
  • myelin is a fatty coating on the axon that makes signals zoom. speedrun mode.
  • the tiny gap between two neurons is a synapse. chemicals called neurotransmitters carry the message across.
  • Dendrites receive input from other neurons.
  • The cell body (soma) contains the nucleus and integrates incoming signals.
  • The axon conducts action potentials away from the cell body.
  • The myelin sheath, formed by oligodendrocytes in the CNS, insulates the axon and increases conduction speed (saltatory conduction).
  • At the synapse, neurotransmitters are released across the synaptic cleft to the next neuron.

gray matterGray matter

mostly neuron cell bodies. it's the outer layer (the cerebral cortex) where most of the big-brain thinking happens.

Composed mainly of neuronal cell bodies, dendrites and synapses. It forms the cerebral cortex and deep nuclei, where information is processed.

white matterWhite matter

bundles of axons wrapped in white, fatty myelin. it sits underneath and is basically the brain's wifi, connecting everything.

Composed of myelinated axons organized into tracts. It lies beneath the cortex and connects brain regions to each other and to the spinal cord.

gyri & sulciGyri & sulci

the bumps are gyri (one is a gyrus) and the grooves are sulci (one is a sulcus). all that folding packs a huge brain into your skull. space-saving queen.

The cortex is folded into ridges (gyri, singular gyrus) and grooves (sulci, singular sulcus). Folding greatly increases cortical surface area within the limited volume of the skull.

04protected like a VIPProtection of the brain

your brain is soft (think tofu), so your body gave it full bodyguard treatment.

Brain tissue is soft and easily injured, so it is protected by several layers.

skullCranium

the cranium is a built-in helmet made of 8 bones fused together. ate.

The cranium consists of 8 bones joined at immovable joints called sutures.

meningesMeninges

three protective layers between skull and brain. outside to inside: dura mater, arachnoid mater, pia mater. remember it as DAP.

Three membranes surround the brain. From outermost to innermost: dura mater, arachnoid mater and pia mater (mnemonic: DAP).

brain juice (CSF)Cerebrospinal fluid

cerebrospinal fluid is a clear liquid your brain floats in. it cushions bumps and makes your brain feel way lighter. built-in airbag.

Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) fills the ventricles and the subarachnoid space. It cushions the brain, reduces its effective weight through buoyancy and helps remove waste.

blood-brain barrierBlood-brain barrier

a super picky filter in the brain's blood vessels. only certain things get in. gatekeeping, but make it healthy.

Tightly joined endothelial cells in brain capillaries form a selective barrier that restricts which substances pass from the blood into brain tissue.
